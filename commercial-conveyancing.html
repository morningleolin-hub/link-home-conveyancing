Commercial Conveyancing

Commercial property transactions can involve more than transferring a title. The property may be leased, subject to a mortgage or easement, used by an operating business, or affected by GST, outgoings, planning and other commercial considerations. The documents and risks differ between retail, office, industrial, warehouse, mixed-use and investment properties. We assist with the conveyancing aspects of commercial property purchases and sales and related lease matters within the agreed scope, helping clients understand the transaction steps and coordinate the required documentation.

Purchasing or selling commercial property

A commercial sale or purchase begins with understanding the asset, the contract terms and the information disclosed about the property. Depending on the matter, review may include title and plan details, easements and covenants, planning information, existing leases or occupancy arrangements, owners corporation information, rates and other outgoings, notices, mortgage arrangements and special conditions. The contract may allocate responsibility for adjustments, risk, deposits, GST and settlement in ways that differ from a standard residential sale. We can review or prepare conveyancing documents within the agreed scope, explain important terms and identify issues that may need specialist legal, accounting, planning or building advice before you commit.

Leases, tenants and income

Where a property is leased, the lease and related records may be central to the transaction. Buyers may need to understand the term, options, rent reviews, permitted use, outgoings, security, incentives, make-good obligations and any disputes or defaults. Vendors may need to provide lease information and coordinate the position at settlement. If the property is subject to a retail lease, the Retail Leases Act 2003 (Vic) may be relevant depending on the premises and circumstances. We can assist with conveyancing and agreed lease-related work, but complex lease negotiations, disputes or advice on the operation of a lease may require a separate scope of legal services.

GST, duty and transaction structure

Commercial property can raise GST questions, including whether a transaction is taxable, input taxed, or potentially eligible for treatment as a going concern. The answer depends on the transaction and the statutory requirements; the contract should not assume a particular GST outcome without appropriate advice. Land transfer duty, foreign purchaser issues, trust or company ownership and tax implications may also need consideration. We can identify conveyancing documents and information relevant to the transaction and recommend that clients obtain accounting or tax advice where appropriate. The website does not provide tax advice and no tax treatment should be assumed from a general description.

Finance, due diligence and settlement

Commercial transactions may involve lender conditions, mortgage discharges, guarantees, caveats, access arrangements, tenancy documents and more detailed settlement adjustments. The timetable should allow sufficient time for the parties and their advisers to provide documents and confirm funds. We can coordinate with the other conveyancer, agent, lender and relevant professionals, prepare transfer and duty information, and organise electronic settlement where applicable. A conveyancer cannot guarantee finance approval, planning suitability, tenant performance or the outcome of specialist investigations.

What to provide

For an initial discussion, provide the property address, whether you are buying or selling, the proposed contract or key terms, details of existing leases, any lender involvement and the expected settlement date. If the transaction relates to an operating business, development project or leased investment, mention that at the outset. We can confirm whether the matter falls within our services, outline the conveyancing steps and explain the proposed fees and disbursements. Each commercial property has its own legal and commercial context, so the appropriate scope depends on the documents and your instructions.
