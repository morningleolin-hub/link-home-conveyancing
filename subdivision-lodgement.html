Subdivision Lodgement

Subdivision can turn one parcel of land into separate lots or alter existing boundaries and interests. In Victoria, a plan must pass through the relevant certification and registration steps before new titles can issue. The process often involves a licensed land surveyor, council or other referral authorities, existing mortgagees and Land Use Victoria. Link Home Conveyancing assists with the conveyancing documentation and plan lodgement stages, including SPEAR lodgements where applicable, helping keep the title and settlement requirements coordinated.

Understanding the plan and project status

Every subdivision has its own plan, conditions and approval pathway. The surveyor generally prepares the plan and manages surveying and technical certification matters, while council and referral authorities may have roles in certification and compliance. Our work focuses on the conveyancing and title-related requirements within the agreed scope. At the outset, we need to understand whether the plan is proposed, certified, awaiting registration or part of a project that is already selling lots. The title, plan details, relevant permits or certification documents, any agreements and the status of conditions help determine what is needed next.

SPEAR and lodgement coordination

SPEAR is used for relevant Victorian plan and subdivision processes. Where instructed and authorised, we can assist with preparing and coordinating the conveyancing information and lodgement steps required for the plan or related instruments. This may involve checking the consistency of party names and title references, arranging supporting documents, communicating with the surveyor and coordinating required consents or dealings. The exact workflow depends on the plan type and the relevant authorities’ requirements. Technical surveying work, planning decisions and certification remain matters for the appropriately qualified professionals and authorities; our role is to handle the conveyancing aspects of the process.

Mortgages, easements and related interests

Existing mortgages can affect whether a plan can be registered and how the resulting titles are dealt with. One or more mortgagees may need to provide consent, partial discharge or other documentation, depending on the land and finance arrangements. A subdivision may also involve easements, covenants, restrictions, service rights, section 173 agreements, boundary realignments or other title matters. These issues should be identified early because they can affect the documents, timing and future use of the lots. We can coordinate the conveyancing information and liaise with the relevant parties, while technical or planning questions may need input from the surveyor, council, engineer or planning adviser.

Registration and sale or settlement of lots

Once the required certification, consents and documents are in place, the plan and related instruments can progress to registration through the applicable Land Use Victoria process. Registration may create new titles and change the way the property is described and transferred. If lots are being sold or refinanced, the timing of registration, lender requirements, contract terms and settlement arrangements may need to be coordinated. We can assist with related transfers and settlements within the agreed scope and help identify dependencies that may affect the project schedule. Registration timing cannot be guaranteed because it depends on complete documentation and the relevant authorities’ processing.

What we need from you

To discuss a subdivision lodgement, provide the property address, current title details, the plan or surveyor’s contact information, the current status of certification, any council conditions or agreements, and details of registered mortgages or caveats. If the project involves staged sales, multiple lots, NICO transfers, partition arrangements or easements, please mention these early. We can then confirm the proposed scope, information required, anticipated coordination and fees. This page describes general conveyancing support and does not replace surveying, planning, engineering or other specialist advice.
