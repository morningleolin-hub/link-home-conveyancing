Transfer of Property Ownership

Guidance through ownership transfers and the documentation involved.

Understanding the arrangement

Property ownership transfers may arise when co-owners change their shares, property passes between family members or spouses, a relationship ends, or an entity or ownership structure changes. The first step is to confirm the current registered owners, the intended new owners, the share being transferred and the reason for the transaction. We may review the title, identification details, mortgage or caveat information and documents explaining the arrangement. Please tell us at the outset whether money will change hands and whether any lender, trust, company or family arrangement is involved.

Documents, duty and registration

We assist with the conveyancing documents and registration process, including preparing transfer documentation, arranging identity verification and client authorisation, coordinating electronic lodgement and communicating with relevant parties. A transfer made without payment is not automatically exempt from Victorian land transfer duty. Any exemption or concession depends on the specific facts and applicable requirements, and supporting documents may be needed. If there is a mortgage, the lender may need to consent or require refinancing before the transfer can proceed. We can identify conveyancing issues and explain what information is required for the proposed transaction.

Plan the transfer carefully

It is helpful to contact us before signing documents or making arrangements that may affect ownership. Changing the names on a title can have consequences for loan arrangements and may require advice from other professionals about matters outside the conveyancing scope. We will explain the steps, documents, expected costs and registration process based on your instructions. To begin, provide the property address, current and proposed ownership details, the reason for the transfer and any available title or lender documents. Each transfer is assessed on its own circumstances; we cannot assume an exemption or a particular outcome before reviewing the relevant information.
