Buying Property

Buying property is a significant financial and legal commitment. In Victoria, the documents and deadlines can differ depending on whether you are purchasing an established home, an apartment, vacant land, a newly built property or an off-the-plan lot. Our role is to help you understand the conveyancing steps, identify matters that may need attention and coordinate the legal and administrative work required to move from contract to settlement.

Before you sign

Where instructed before signing, we can review the Contract of Sale and Section 32 Vendor Statement and explain the practical effect of relevant clauses and disclosures. This may include the title details, easements and covenants, planning information, owners corporation material, settlement date, deposit arrangements, special conditions and any conditions relating to finance or inspections. If you are buying at auction, the contract is generally binding when the successful bid is accepted, so it is important to have the documents reviewed and your arrangements in place before bidding. We can identify questions to raise with the agent or vendor’s representative, and discuss whether proposed amendments or additional enquiries should be considered. A document review cannot replace building, pest, financial, planning or other specialist investigations where those are relevant.

Due diligence and transaction planning

A property may carry obligations or restrictions that are not obvious from an inspection. Depending on the property and your instructions, relevant enquiries may include title and plan searches, rates and water information, planning controls, owners corporation certificates, notices or other property-specific records. We can help explain the conveyancing implications of the information obtained and flag issues that may warrant further advice from a planner, building consultant, accountant, lender or other specialist. We can also discuss the practical requirements for signing, identity verification, electronic conveyancing and payment of funds. If you are purchasing with another person, through a company or trustee, or using a particular ownership structure, tell us early so the proposed purchaser name and transaction documents can be considered before signing.

After the contract is signed

Once the contract is unconditional or the relevant conditions have been satisfied, we coordinate the steps needed to prepare for settlement. This can include reviewing the contract dates, liaising with the vendor’s conveyancer, agent and lender, preparing transfer and duty information, arranging electronic settlement through the applicable platform, and confirming the adjustments and funds required. If finance is involved, your lender may have its own approval, signing and funding deadlines. We help keep the conveyancing timetable visible, but lending approval and the release of funds remain matters for the lender and borrower.
