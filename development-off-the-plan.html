Property Development & Off-the-Plan

Property development and off-the-plan transactions involve several moving parts: land ownership, project structure, planning and subdivision milestones, contract documentation, purchaser disclosures, finance, construction progress and eventual settlement. The legal and conveyancing requirements vary depending on whether the project involves vacant land, a townhouse or apartment development, staged releases, or a purchase before a plan of subdivision is registered. We provide conveyancing support for developers and purchasers within the agreed scope, with a focus on document coordination, clear communication and key dates.

Developer-side documentation

For a developer selling new lots or dwellings, preparation and coordination of sale documents should begin early. The contract and Section 32 Vendor Statement need to reflect the property and the information required for the particular sale. Depending on the project, relevant material may include a proposed plan of subdivision, planning information, proposed owners corporation arrangements, easements and restrictions, services information, building specifications or finishes schedules, disclosures about the development and provisions dealing with changes or delays. The documents must be tailored to the project and applicable requirements; a template should not be assumed suitable for every development. We can assist with the conveyancing documentation and coordinate information from the developer, surveyor and other advisers.

Purchaser-side off-the-plan review

An off-the-plan purchaser may be committing to buy a property before the final title exists or before construction is complete. The contract can deal with deposit arrangements, the anticipated plan, permitted variations, finishes, settlement triggers, sunset dates and other matters that affect the purchaser’s rights and obligations. We can review the contract and vendor statement, explain key clauses in practical terms and identify issues that may need negotiation or independent advice. Purchasers should understand which matters are fixed and which may change under the contract, how settlement is triggered and what steps they must take before settlement. Building quality, valuation, finance suitability and tax outcomes may require separate professional advice.

Project milestones and coordination

Development matters often depend on events outside the conveyancer’s direct control, including planning or certification decisions, construction progress, plan registration, lender approvals and completion documentation. We can help map the conveyancing steps and key contractual dates, coordinate documents with the relevant parties and identify information needed before sales or settlements proceed. If a project includes multiple lots or staged releases, consistency between contract documents, lot details and title information is important. Early coordination can reduce the risk of avoidable administrative delays, although no particular approval or registration date can be guaranteed.

Settlement and multiple-lot transactions

As titles become available and settlement approaches, the parties may need to coordinate registration, lender consents, duty information, electronic settlement, adjustments and transfer documents. For developers, this may involve multiple settlements over a period; for purchasers, it may involve satisfying contract requirements and arranging finance and funds. We can assist with the conveyancing process within the agreed scope and communicate outstanding requirements to the relevant parties. More complex structures, funding arrangements, tax matters or development agreements may require specialist legal, accounting or financial advice.

Starting a conversation

Useful starting information includes the project location, whether you act for the developer or purchaser, the current contract or draft documents, the plan or title status, the anticipated milestones and the number of lots involved. Tell us about any staged settlement arrangements, owners corporation, easements, special conditions or financing dependencies. We can then discuss the work required, information needed and proposed fees. Our services are limited to the agreed conveyancing scope and do not replace planning, building, engineering, tax or financial advice.
