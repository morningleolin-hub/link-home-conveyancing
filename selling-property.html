Selling Property

Selling property in Victoria involves preparing accurate disclosure documents, agreeing the contract terms and coordinating the steps that lead to settlement. Good preparation can help reduce avoidable delays and make it easier for your selling agent to answer prospective purchasers’ questions. We assist vendors with the conveyancing documentation and transaction administration from preparation through to completion, taking account of the property type and the terms of the proposed sale.

Preparing the contract and Section 32 Vendor Statement

Before a property is offered for sale, the vendor generally needs a Contract of Sale and a Section 32 Vendor Statement that complies with the applicable Victorian requirements. The information required depends on the property and the vendor’s circumstances. Relevant documents may include title and plan information, mortgages and other interests, easements or covenants, planning information, rates and charges, owners corporation records, building or permit information where applicable, and details of services or notices. We can help gather the required information, prepare or review the sale documents and identify gaps that need to be addressed before the documents are provided to purchasers. The vendor remains responsible for giving complete and accurate information, so it is important to disclose known facts and respond carefully to requests for information.

Contract terms and sale method

The contract should reflect the agreed transaction, including the purchase price, deposit, settlement date, inclusions and exclusions, and any special conditions. Private sales and auctions may involve different practical considerations. A sale by auction is generally unconditional once the contract is signed following the successful bid, so documents and arrangements should be checked before auction day. For a private sale, any finance, building, pest or other conditions need to be clearly drafted and understood by the parties. We can discuss the conveyancing implications of proposed terms and liaise with the selling agent or purchaser’s representative about the documentation. If the property is tenanted, part of an owners corporation, vacant land or subject to a development arrangement, tell us at the outset so relevant matters can be considered.

From signing to settlement

After the contract is signed, we monitor the contractual dates and coordinate the steps needed for completion. This may include receiving the deposit details, responding to reasonable conveyancing enquiries, obtaining mortgage payout figures, arranging discharge of any registered mortgage, preparing transfer and duty information, and liaising with the purchaser’s conveyancer and any lender. We also calculate or check settlement adjustments for relevant rates, taxes, water charges, rent or other items according to the contract and available information. If there is a mortgage, the lender’s discharge process can require notice and documentation, so early communication is helpful.

Settlement and completion

As settlement approaches, we confirm the required documents, figures and electronic settlement arrangements. Settlement typically involves payment of the balance of the price, completion of the agreed financial adjustments and lodgement of the transfer and mortgage discharge where applicable. We confirm the outcome and communicate with the relevant parties so that the agent can manage the release of keys in accordance with the contract. If an issue arises—such as a delay in mortgage discharge, an outstanding document or a proposed change to settlement—we explain the conveyancing options available and the need for instructions or separate legal advice where required.

What vendors can do early

Please provide the property address, title or prior purchase documents if available, details of any mortgage, the proposed sale method and the agent’s anticipated campaign dates. Tell us about any owners corporation, tenancy, building works, subdivision, easement, caveat or other matter that may affect disclosure. Early preparation can help identify missing information before the sale timetable becomes urgent. We will explain the scope of work and fees before commencing. Each sale depends on its own title, disclosure requirements and contract terms, and this website is general information rather than advice on a particular property.
