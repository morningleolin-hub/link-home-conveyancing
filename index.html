Buying Property

Buying property is a significant financial and legal commitment. In Victoria, the documents and deadlines can differ depending on whether you are purchasing an established home, an apartment, vacant land, a newly built property or an off-the-plan lot. Our role is to help you understand the conveyancing steps, identify matters that may need attention and coordinate the legal and administrative work required to move from contract to settlement.

Before you sign

Where instructed before signing, we can review the Contract of Sale and Section 32 Vendor Statement and explain the practical effect of relevant clauses and disclosures. This may include the title details, easements and covenants, planning information, owners corporation material, settlement date, deposit arrangements, special conditions and any conditions relating to finance or inspections. If you are buying at auction, the contract is generally binding when the successful bid is accepted, so it is important to have the documents reviewed and your arrangements in place before bidding. We can identify questions to raise with the agent or vendor’s representative, and discuss whether proposed amendments or additional enquiries should be considered. A document review cannot replace building, pest, financial, planning or other specialist investigations where those are relevant.

Due diligence and transaction planning

A property may carry obligations or restrictions that are not obvious from an inspection. Depending on the property and your instructions, relevant enquiries may include title and plan searches, rates and water information, planning controls, owners corporation certificates, notices or other property-specific records. We can help explain the conveyancing implications of the information obtained and flag issues that may warrant further advice from a planner, building consultant, accountant, lender or other specialist. We can also discuss the practical requirements for signing, identity verification, electronic conveyancing and payment of funds. If you are purchasing with another person, through a company or trustee, or using a particular ownership structure, tell us early so the proposed purchaser name and transaction documents can be considered before signing.

After the contract is signed

Once the contract is unconditional or the relevant conditions have been satisfied, we coordinate the steps needed to prepare for settlement. This can include reviewing the contract dates, liaising with the vendor’s conveyancer, agent and lender, preparing transfer and duty information, arranging electronic settlement through the applicable platform, and confirming the adjustments and funds required. If finance is involved, your lender may have its own approval, signing and funding deadlines. We help keep the conveyancing timetable visible, but lending approval and the release of funds remain matters for the lender and borrower.

Settlement and after settlement

Before settlement, we work through the documents and settlement figures, confirm that the parties are ready and address outstanding conveyancing requirements. At settlement, funds are exchanged and the transfer is lodged for registration through the relevant electronic process where applicable. We then confirm completion and explain any next administrative steps, such as collecting keys through the agent, confirming registration or retaining the final documents. The exact process depends on the contract, property and lender arrangements.

What to prepare

If you are considering a purchase, useful starting information includes the property address, the proposed contract and Section 32 (if available), the expected purchase price, intended purchaser names, whether finance is required and your preferred settlement timeframe. Let us know if the property is an apartment, part of an owners corporation, vacant land, a new build or an off-the-plan purchase, as those circumstances may raise different questions. We will explain the proposed scope of work and costs before proceeding. Every property and contract is different; the appropriate advice depends on the documents and your circumstances.

Selling Property

Selling property in Victoria involves preparing accurate disclosure documents, agreeing the contract terms and coordinating the steps that lead to settlement. Good preparation can help reduce avoidable delays and make it easier for your selling agent to answer prospective purchasers’ questions. We assist vendors with the conveyancing documentation and transaction administration from preparation through to completion, taking account of the property type and the terms of the proposed sale.

Preparing the contract and Section 32 Vendor Statement

Before a property is offered for sale, the vendor generally needs a Contract of Sale and a Section 32 Vendor Statement that complies with the applicable Victorian requirements. The information required depends on the property and the vendor’s circumstances. Relevant documents may include title and plan information, mortgages and other interests, easements or covenants, planning information, rates and charges, owners corporation records, building or permit information where applicable, and details of services or notices. We can help gather the required information, prepare or review the sale documents and identify gaps that need to be addressed before the documents are provided to purchasers. The vendor remains responsible for giving complete and accurate information, so it is important to disclose known facts and respond carefully to requests for information.

Contract terms and sale method

The contract should reflect the agreed transaction, including the purchase price, deposit, settlement date, inclusions and exclusions, and any special conditions. Private sales and auctions may involve different practical considerations. A sale by auction is generally unconditional once the contract is signed following the successful bid, so documents and arrangements should be checked before auction day. For a private sale, any finance, building, pest or other conditions need to be clearly drafted and understood by the parties. We can discuss the conveyancing implications of proposed terms and liaise with the selling agent or purchaser’s representative about the documentation. If the property is tenanted, part of an owners corporation, vacant land or subject to a development arrangement, tell us at the outset so relevant matters can be considered.

From signing to settlement

After the contract is signed, we monitor the contractual dates and coordinate the steps needed for completion. This may include receiving the deposit details, responding to reasonable conveyancing enquiries, obtaining mortgage payout figures, arranging discharge of any registered mortgage, preparing transfer and duty information, and liaising with the purchaser’s conveyancer and any lender. We also calculate or check settlement adjustments for relevant rates, taxes, water charges, rent or other items according to the contract and available information. If there is a mortgage, the lender’s discharge process can require notice and documentation, so early communication is helpful.

Settlement and completion

As settlement approaches, we confirm the required documents, figures and electronic settlement arrangements. Settlement typically involves payment of the balance of the price, completion of the agreed financial adjustments and lodgement of the transfer and mortgage discharge where applicable. We confirm the outcome and communicate with the relevant parties so that the agent can manage the release of keys in accordance with the contract. If an issue arises—such as a delay in mortgage discharge, an outstanding document or a proposed change to settlement—we explain the conveyancing options available and the need for instructions or separate legal advice where required.

What vendors can do early

Please provide the property address, title or prior purchase documents if available, details of any mortgage, the proposed sale method and the agent’s anticipated campaign dates. Tell us about any owners corporation, tenancy, building works, subdivision, easement, caveat or other matter that may affect disclosure. Early preparation can help identify missing information before the sale timetable becomes urgent. We will explain the scope of work and fees before commencing. Each sale depends on its own title, disclosure requirements and contract terms, and this website is general information rather than advice on a particular property.

Transfer of Property Ownership

A transfer of property ownership may be needed when ownership shares change, a property is transferred between family members, spouses or related entities, or a title needs to be updated following a particular life or business event. Although some transfers are described as gifts or made without payment, the legal, duty, tax and lending consequences depend on the circumstances and the way the transaction is structured. We assist with the conveyancing documents and registration process and help identify issues that should be checked before the transfer proceeds.

Understanding the proposed transfer

The first step is to understand who currently owns the property, who is intended to become the registered proprietor, what share is changing and why the transfer is proposed. We may need to review a current title, the parties’ identification details, any mortgage or caveat, and documents explaining the arrangement. Transfers can arise from changes between co-owners, family arrangements, relationship breakdown, estate-related events, trust or company structures, or a change in the proportions held by proprietors. Different circumstances can require different supporting documents or procedures. It is important to explain the actual arrangement rather than relying only on a label such as “gift”, “nomination” or “no consideration”.

Duty, tax and finance considerations

A transfer without cash consideration is not automatically exempt from Victorian land transfer duty. The State Revenue Office may require a duty assessment and supporting information, and any exemption or concession depends on the applicable legislation and the facts. Capital gains tax, land tax, foreign purchaser issues, trust or company consequences and other tax questions may also arise, but these are not determined solely by the conveyancing documents. We can help identify when specialist tax advice or a State Revenue Office assessment should be obtained. If a mortgage is registered, the lender’s consent, refinance, discharge or replacement loan arrangements may be required. The lender must be involved early because changing the registered ownership without dealing with the mortgage arrangements may not be possible or appropriate.

Documents and registration

Depending on the transaction, the process may involve a transfer of land, verification of identity, client authorisation, digital duties information, supporting declarations, lender documents and electronic lodgement. The parties’ names and capacity must be correct—for example, an individual, company or trustee capacity should not be assumed interchangeable. We coordinate the conveyancing information and work with the other parties and relevant service providers to prepare the transaction for lodgement. If the transfer is connected with a separation, estate, trust, SMSF or company arrangement, we may need to review relevant orders, agreements, deeds or corporate documents and may recommend that the parties obtain independent legal or tax advice.

Timing and practical issues

A transfer can take longer where the parties’ instructions are incomplete, the title contains a mortgage or caveat, the lender needs to approve a change, duty information is outstanding, or additional documents are required. Before work begins, we can outline the expected steps, information needed and likely disbursements. We will not assume that a transfer is duty-free or that no tax consequences arise without an appropriate basis. If the proposed transfer is part of a broader agreement—such as a property settlement or business restructure—tell us so that the conveyancing process can be coordinated with the relevant professional advisers.

What to provide

Useful starting documents include a current title or property address, the full legal names of all current and proposed owners, the proposed ownership shares, details of consideration or any gift arrangement, mortgage information and the reason for the transfer. Provide any relevant court order, binding financial agreement, trust deed, company documents or estate documents if applicable. The exact requirements depend on the facts. Our role is to assist with the conveyancing and registration process within the agreed scope and to identify when further legal, financial or tax advice is appropriate.

Subdivision Lodgement

Subdivision can turn one parcel of land into separate lots or alter existing boundaries and interests. In Victoria, a plan must pass through the relevant certification and registration steps before new titles can issue. The process often involves a licensed land surveyor, council or other referral authorities, existing mortgagees and Land Use Victoria. Link Home Conveyancing assists with the conveyancing documentation and plan lodgement stages, including SPEAR lodgements where applicable, helping keep the title and settlement requirements coordinated.

Understanding the plan and project status

Every subdivision has its own plan, conditions and approval pathway. The surveyor generally prepares the plan and manages surveying and technical certification matters, while council and referral authorities may have roles in certification and compliance. Our work focuses on the conveyancing and title-related requirements within the agreed scope. At the outset, we need to understand whether the plan is proposed, certified, awaiting registration or part of a project that is already selling lots. The title, plan details, relevant permits or certification documents, any agreements and the status of conditions help determine what is needed next.

SPEAR and lodgement coordination

SPEAR is used for relevant Victorian plan and subdivision processes. Where instructed and authorised, we can assist with preparing and coordinating the conveyancing information and lodgement steps required for the plan or related instruments. This may involve checking the consistency of party names and title references, arranging supporting documents, communicating with the surveyor and coordinating required consents or dealings. The exact workflow depends on the plan type and the relevant authorities’ requirements. Technical surveying work, planning decisions and certification remain matters for the appropriately qualified professionals and authorities; our role is to handle the conveyancing aspects of the process.

Mortgages, easements and related interests

Existing mortgages can affect whether a plan can be registered and how the resulting titles are dealt with. One or more mortgagees may need to provide consent, partial discharge or other documentation, depending on the land and finance arrangements. A subdivision may also involve easements, covenants, restrictions, service rights, section 173 agreements, boundary realignments or other title matters. These issues should be identified early because they can affect the documents, timing and future use of the lots. We can coordinate the conveyancing information and liaise with the relevant parties, while technical or planning questions may need input from the surveyor, council, engineer or planning adviser.

Registration and sale or settlement of lots

Once the required certification, consents and documents are in place, the plan and related instruments can progress to registration through the applicable Land Use Victoria process. Registration may create new titles and change the way the property is described and transferred. If lots are being sold or refinanced, the timing of registration, lender requirements, contract terms and settlement arrangements may need to be coordinated. We can assist with related transfers and settlements within the agreed scope and help identify dependencies that may affect the project schedule. Registration timing cannot be guaranteed because it depends on complete documentation and the relevant authorities’ processing.

What we need from you

To discuss a subdivision lodgement, provide the property address, current title details, the plan or surveyor’s contact information, the current status of certification, any council conditions or agreements, and details of registered mortgages or caveats. If the project involves staged sales, multiple lots, NICO transfers, partition arrangements or easements, please mention these early. We can then confirm the proposed scope, information required, anticipated coordination and fees. This page describes general conveyancing support and does not replace surveying, planning, engineering or other specialist advice.

Property Development & Off-the-Plan

Property development and off-the-plan transactions involve several moving parts: land ownership, project structure, planning and subdivision milestones, contract documentation, purchaser disclosures, finance, construction progress and eventual settlement. The legal and conveyancing requirements vary depending on whether the project involves vacant land, a townhouse or apartment development, staged releases, or a purchase before a plan of subdivision is registered. We provide conveyancing support for developers and purchasers within the agreed scope, with a focus on document coordination, clear communication and key dates.

Developer-side documentation

For a developer selling new lots or dwellings, preparation and coordination of sale documents should begin early. The contract and Section 32 Vendor Statement need to reflect the property and the information required for the particular sale. Depending on the project, relevant material may include a proposed plan of subdivision, planning information, proposed owners corporation arrangements, easements and restrictions, services information, building specifications or finishes schedules, disclosures about the development and provisions dealing with changes or delays. The documents must be tailored to the project and applicable requirements; a template should not be assumed suitable for every development. We can assist with the conveyancing documentation and coordinate information from the developer, surveyor and other advisers.

Purchaser-side off-the-plan review

An off-the-plan purchaser may be committing to buy a property before the final title exists or before construction is complete. The contract can deal with deposit arrangements, the anticipated plan, permitted variations, finishes, settlement triggers, sunset dates and other matters that affect the purchaser’s rights and obligations. We can review the contract and vendor statement, explain key clauses in practical terms and identify issues that may need negotiation or independent advice. Purchasers should understand which matters are fixed and which may change under the contract, how settlement is triggered and what steps they must take before settlement. Building quality, valuation, finance suitability and tax outcomes may require separate professional advice.

Project milestones and coordination

Development matters often depend on events outside the conveyancer’s direct control, including planning or certification decisions, construction progress, plan registration, lender approvals and completion documentation. We can help map the conveyancing steps and key contractual dates, coordinate documents with the relevant parties and identify information needed before sales or settlements proceed. If a project includes multiple lots or staged releases, consistency between contract documents, lot details and title information is important. Early coordination can reduce the risk of avoidable administrative delays, although no particular approval or registration date can be guaranteed.

Settlement and multiple-lot transactions

As titles become available and settlement approaches, the parties may need to coordinate registration, lender consents, duty information, electronic settlement, adjustments and transfer documents. For developers, this may involve multiple settlements over a period; for purchasers, it may involve satisfying contract requirements and arranging finance and funds. We can assist with the conveyancing process within the agreed scope and communicate outstanding requirements to the relevant parties. More complex structures, funding arrangements, tax matters or development agreements may require specialist legal, accounting or financial advice.

Starting a conversation

Useful starting information includes the project location, whether you act for the developer or purchaser, the current contract or draft documents, the plan or title status, the anticipated milestones and the number of lots involved. Tell us about any staged settlement arrangements, owners corporation, easements, special conditions or financing dependencies. We can then discuss the work required, information needed and proposed fees. Our services are limited to the agreed conveyancing scope and do not replace planning, building, engineering, tax or financial advice.

Commercial Conveyancing

Commercial property transactions can involve more than transferring a title. The property may be leased, subject to a mortgage or easement, used by an operating business, or affected by GST, outgoings, planning and other commercial considerations. The documents and risks differ between retail, office, industrial, warehouse, mixed-use and investment properties. We assist with the conveyancing aspects of commercial property purchases and sales and related lease matters within the agreed scope, helping clients understand the transaction steps and coordinate the required documentation.

Purchasing or selling commercial property

A commercial sale or purchase begins with understanding the asset, the contract terms and the information disclosed about the property. Depending on the matter, review may include title and plan details, easements and covenants, planning information, existing leases or occupancy arrangements, owners corporation information, rates and other outgoings, notices, mortgage arrangements and special conditions. The contract may allocate responsibility for adjustments, risk, deposits, GST and settlement in ways that differ from a standard residential sale. We can review or prepare conveyancing documents within the agreed scope, explain important terms and identify issues that may need specialist legal, accounting, planning or building advice before you commit.

Leases, tenants and income

Where a property is leased, the lease and related records may be central to the transaction. Buyers may need to understand the term, options, rent reviews, permitted use, outgoings, security, incentives, make-good obligations and any disputes or defaults. Vendors may need to provide lease information and coordinate the position at settlement. If the property is subject to a retail lease, the Retail Leases Act 2003 (Vic) may be relevant depending on the premises and circumstances. We can assist with conveyancing and agreed lease-related work, but complex lease negotiations, disputes or advice on the operation of a lease may require a separate scope of legal services.

GST, duty and transaction structure

Commercial property can raise GST questions, including whether a transaction is taxable, input taxed, or potentially eligible for treatment as a going concern. The answer depends on the transaction and the statutory requirements; the contract should not assume a particular GST outcome without appropriate advice. Land transfer duty, foreign purchaser issues, trust or company ownership and tax implications may also need consideration. We can identify conveyancing documents and information relevant to the transaction and recommend that clients obtain accounting or tax advice where appropriate. The website does not provide tax advice and no tax treatment should be assumed from a general description.

Finance, due diligence and settlement

Commercial transactions may involve lender conditions, mortgage discharges, guarantees, caveats, access arrangements, tenancy documents and more detailed settlement adjustments. The timetable should allow sufficient time for the parties and their advisers to provide documents and confirm funds. We can coordinate with the other conveyancer, agent, lender and relevant professionals, prepare transfer and duty information, and organise electronic settlement where applicable. A conveyancer cannot guarantee finance approval, planning suitability, tenant performance or the outcome of specialist investigations.

What to provide

For an initial discussion, provide the property address, whether you are buying or selling, the proposed contract or key terms, details of existing leases, any lender involvement and the expected settlement date. If the transaction relates to an operating business, development project or leased investment, mention that at the outset. We can confirm whether the matter falls within our services, outline the conveyancing steps and explain the proposed fees and disbursements. Each commercial property has its own legal and commercial context, so the appropriate scope depends on the documents and your instructions.
